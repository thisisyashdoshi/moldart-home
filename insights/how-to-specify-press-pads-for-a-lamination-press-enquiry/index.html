Press Pads · RFQ / Evaluation Guide

How to Specify Press Pads for a Lamination Press Enquiry

A press-pad enquiry is more useful when the exact pad construction, press stack and operating conditions are defined together. Dimensions and performance values should be confirmed for the actual product, application and process rather than assumed from a generic grade name.

Important: this is a requirement-capture framework, not a universal MOLDART specification for cycle life, temperature, specific pressure, thermal conductivity, recovery, wire-count class, cleanliness limit or application assignment.

Use this checklist to prepare a clearer press-pad RFQ or technical evaluation. Where an incumbent pad, approved baseline or controlled test method already exists, include it so comparative performance can be reviewed under stated conditions.

01

Identify the exact pad construction and traceability

State the required press-pad type or construction where known and identify the product revision, supplier reference or incumbent baseline used for comparison. Do not assume that visually similar pads have equivalent construction or performance.

02

Define nominal dimensions and thickness measurement

Provide nominal length, width and thickness together with the thickness measurement method, measurement grid, applicable tolerance/disposition basis and any relevant edge-condition requirement. No universal pad thickness or dimensional tolerance is implied by this guide.

03

Define the press, application and stack

State the intended press/application, pressing area, stack arrangement and relevant panel or laminate system. Include the incumbent operating baseline where available so any comparison is made against the actual production context.

04

Record the actual temperature and pressure basis

Share the actual temperature profile relevant to the press cycle. Distinguish hydraulic gauge pressure from the calculated specific pressure applied over the pressing area; they are not interchangeable values.

05

Define thermal and compressibility evaluation methods

Where thermal response, compressibility or recovery matters, specify the evaluation method, comparator, temperature/pressure condition and acceptance basis. Comparative laboratory methods may support review where technically appropriate, but idealized results should not be treated as direct proof of production performance.

06

Define durability and cleanliness requirements

For durability, use application-specific heat-aging or cyclic testing and, where practical, a controlled field trial. State cleanliness, outgassing or migration requirements only where they are relevant to the process and can be measured against an agreed method.

07

Define visual integrity, sampling and final disposition

Include construction-integrity checks, sampling/inspection basis and a clear PASS / FAIL / OPEN disposition method with evidence, date and reviewer. This avoids treating a sample receipt or isolated test value as final qualification.

Prepare the press-pad enquiry for technical review

Share the pad construction/reference, nominal dimensions, press/application/stack, temperature profile, specific-pressure basis, evaluation methods, quantity and any incumbent comparison data available.

FAQ

Common press-pad evaluation questions

Is a cycle-life claim enough to qualify a press pad?

No. Cycle life depends on the exact pad construction, press conditions, temperature and pressure history, application, cleanliness and acceptance method. Qualification should use product- and process-specific evidence.

Should hydraulic gauge pressure be treated as the same as specific pressure on the panel?

No. The enquiry should distinguish the press hydraulic reading from the calculated specific pressure relevant to the pressing area and application.

Can a laboratory thermal result be used directly as production proof?

Not automatically. Comparative laboratory methods can support evaluation where technically appropriate, but idealized results should be interpreted against the actual press stack and production conditions.
