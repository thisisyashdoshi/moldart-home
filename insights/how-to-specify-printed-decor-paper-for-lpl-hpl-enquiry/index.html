How to Specify Printed Decor Paper for an LPL / HPL Enquiry

Decor paper is not qualified by GSM alone. A useful enquiry defines the exact paper grade, printing and impregnation route, colour/opacity basis, converting requirements and the test methods that matter for the intended laminate programme.

Important: this is a requirement-capture framework, not a universal MOLDART specification for basis weight, tensile strength, moisture, opacity, resin uptake, dimensional stability or any other numerical acceptance value.

Current major decor-paper manufacturers publish materially different product ranges and processing characteristics, which is why the exact paper grade and supplier datasheet should control qualification rather than a generic industry-wide number. This checklist is designed to make RFQs and technical comparisons clearer.

01

Identify the exact paper type and intended process

State whether the requirement is for print-base/decor paper intended for resin impregnation and the relevant LPL/HPL route. Identify the supplier grade, product family or incumbent reference where available.

02

Specify basis weight and tolerance for the exact grade

Provide the target basis weight and its tolerance as an order-specific requirement. Do not assume one universal GSM band across all decor-paper products, printing systems, impregnation processes or laminate applications.

03

Define colour and opacity expectations

State the colour standard/reference, acceptable variation method and any opacity requirement that matters to the printed decor and finished laminate. Use a defined measurement or approved physical reference where practical.

04

Define printability requirements

State the intended printing process, ink system and relevant print-quality expectations. Surface quality, colour uptake, ink holdout/penetration and machine runnability should be evaluated against the actual printing route rather than assumed from paper appearance alone.

05

Define impregnation and resin-penetration behaviour

Record the intended resin/process conditions and the relevant impregnation or resin-penetration acceptance method. Qualification should be based on the exact paper grade and process, not a generic claim that all decor papers behave the same way.

06

Define dimensional stability, moisture and runnability

Where relevant, state the dimensional-stability basis, moisture range/method and mechanical or runnability requirements needed for printing, impregnation and converting. Numerical limits should come from the selected product specification and defined test methods.

07

Define tensile / wet-strength methods before setting numbers

A tensile or wet-strength value is not meaningful without the test method, specimen width and machine/cross direction, conditioning and acceptance evidence. Do not use an isolated number as a universal buying criterion.

08

Close with the exact inspection and approval basis

Attach the approved specification, test methods, sample/reference standard and final PASS / FAIL / OPEN disposition method. This keeps supplier claims, incoming QC and production evaluation aligned to the same order-specific evidence.

Prepare the printed decor paper enquiry for technical review

Share the paper type/grade, target basis weight, colour/opacity basis, printing process, resin/impregnation route, dimensional/moisture requirements, test methods, quantity and any approved incumbent sample or specification.

FAQ

Common printed decor paper qualification questions

Is one universal GSM range suitable for all decor-paper programmes?

No. Basis weight is product- and process-specific. The enquiry should state the exact target basis weight and tolerance against the selected supplier grade, printing process, impregnation route and end application.

Is a wet-tensile number meaningful without a test method?

Not by itself. A tensile or wet-strength requirement should identify the method, specimen width and direction, conditioning and acceptance basis for the exact paper grade.

What matters besides basis weight?

Colour and opacity, printability, resin penetration and impregnation behaviour, dimensional stability, moisture, mechanical/runnability properties and the exact test methods can all be relevant depending on the programme.
